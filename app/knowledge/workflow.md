# Workflow, tempo and key

## Using reference tracks well
tags: reference, references, comparison, workflow, matched loudness, a/b, listening

Choose two or three commercial releases in the same genre and with a similar arrangement density. Import them into the session on a track that bypasses the master chain, match loudness before comparing (the louder one always seems better), and compare section by section: drop with drop, verse with verse. Focus on one aspect at a time: low-end weight, vocal level, brightness, width. Use the numbers to confirm what you hear, not to replace listening.

## Tempo, key and harmonic mixing
tags: bpm, tempo, key, mode, harmonic mixing, camelot, dj, sample, pitch, tuning

Knowing the tempo and key helps you pick compatible samples and references, write basslines that sit in the key, and tune 808s and kicks to the root note of the song so the low end doesn't clash. DJs mix tracks whose keys are adjacent on the Camelot wheel. Automatic key detection can confuse relative major and minor keys (C major and A minor share notes) and can be thrown off by atonal or heavily percussive material, so treat a low-confidence result as a hint.

## Mix translation checks
tags: translation, monitoring, headphones, car, phone, mono, check, small speakers

Before calling a mix finished, check it on several systems: studio monitors, headphones, a phone speaker, a car, and in mono. Phones reveal whether the bass has enough harmonics to be heard; mono reveals phase problems; cars reveal low-end excess. Listen at low volume too: if the balance holds when quiet, it will hold when loud. Take notes on each system and change only what is wrong on most of them.

## Gain staging and headroom before mastering
tags: headroom, gain staging, mixdown, mastering prep, peak level, bounce, delivery

When sending a mix to mastering, leave the master bus without the final limiter, peak around -6 to -3 dBFS, render at the session sample rate in 24-bit or 32-bit float, and leave a little silence at the start and end. If you rely on bus processing (glue compression, saturation) for the sound, keep it on but tell the mastering engineer. Also send a version with your limiter so they know the loudness you are aiming for.
